          📖 How to play chess

          Learn every piece on the real board, one lesson at a time, then play a practice game against a bot.

          The goal

          Trap the other player's king. When a king is attacked and cannot escape, that is checkmate and the game is over. Each side starts with 16 pieces: one king, one queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. You take turns, one move each, and the blue pieces (the side that starts at the bottom of the board) move first.

          How the pieces move
          • King: one square in any direction. Protect it at all costs.
          • Queen: any number of squares in a straight line, sideways, up/down or diagonally. The strongest piece.
          • Rook: any number of squares sideways or up/down.
          • Bishop: any number of squares diagonally. A bishop always stays on the same color squares.
          • Knight: an "L": two squares one way, then one square to the side. It is the only piece that can jump over others.
          • Pawn: one square forward (two from its starting square). It captures one square diagonally forward, never straight.

          Pieces cannot jump over each other (except the knight). To capture, move onto a square holding an enemy piece and it is removed.

          Special moves
          • Castling: move your king two squares toward a rook, and the rook hops to the other side of the king. Allowed only if neither piece has moved, nothing is in between, and the king is not in check or passing through check.
          • En passant: if an enemy pawn jumps two squares and lands right beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved only one square. You must do it on the very next move.
          • Promotion: a pawn that reaches the far end of the board turns into a queen, rook, bishop or knight of your choice (most players pick a queen).
          Check, checkmate and draws
          • Check: your king is under attack. You must get out of it: move the king, block the attack, or capture the attacker.
          • Checkmate: check with no way out. The player who delivers it wins.
          • Stalemate: it is your turn, you are not in check, but you have no legal move. The game is a draw.
          • Other draws: both players agree, the same position happens three times, 50 moves pass without a capture or pawn move, or neither side has enough pieces left to checkmate.
          • The clock: each game has a 5 or 10 minute clock per player. If your time runs out, you lose.
          Playing Sproto Chess
          • Move: tap a piece, then tap the square to move it to.
          • Start a game: pick 5, 10 or 15 minutes and tap Start game, then invite a friend or wait for someone to join from Open games.
          • Join or watch: tap Join on an open game, or watch a live game from Live games. Spectators can chat with the players and cheer for a side.
          • During a game: use live chat, offer a draw, resign, or draw arrows on the board. Pick your board colors any time.
          • After a game: ask for a rematch, or go back to the lobby. Wins, losses and draws count on the public leaderboard.
          Quick tips for beginners
          • Control the center squares with your pawns and knights early.
          • Bring out your knights and bishops before moving the queen.
          • Castle early to keep your king safe.
          • Before every move, ask: "What is my opponent threatening?"
          • Do not give away pieces for free. A queen is worth about 9 pawns, a rook 5, a bishop or knight 3.
